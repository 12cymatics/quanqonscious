The bound

Why nothing sits left of 2

Take the two points A and B furthest apart on the curve — their separation is the diameter D. They cut the curve into two arcs, and each arc joins A to B, so each is at least as long as the straight line between them.

Two arcs, each ≥ D. So P ≥ 2D for every closed curve, with equality only in the degenerate flat limit. Thom Egg II was published at P/D = 1.7124. There is no shape it could describe.

The method swap

The fix was worse than the bug

E(m) was a 2048-interval Simpson rule. Replacing it with the arithmetic-geometric mean should have been a straight win — quadratic convergence, six iterations, no interval count silently setting the error. The first AGM version was less accurate than the quadrature it replaced.

Once a and b agree to within an ulp, c = (a−b)/2 stops shrinking and plateaus near 5.6e-17. An absolute cutoff of 1e-18 is never reached, so the loop runs to its 64-iteration cap while power doubles to 2⁶⁴ — and the terms 2ⁿc² start growing again. Terminating relatively, on |c| > ε·a, fixes it.
