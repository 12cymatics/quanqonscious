## Foundation and scope

Begin every substantive system design with the user's supplied ancient-mathematics, Vedic and geometric framework. Identify the exact definition, source, domain, invariant and derivation that connect it to the proposed mechanic. Use the shared mathematical-integrity contract. Historical inspiration, a formal theorem, a simulation result and a measured physical effect are different kinds of evidence. Never turn one into another through naming or visual presentation.

Distinguish three layers explicitly: narrative symbolism, authored gameplay and physically calibrated simulation. Geometric or astronomical themes may guide a fictional world without being presented as evidence of historical technology, medical benefit or measurable human transformation. Identify recorded data sources and their uncertainty. Do not invent archaeological dimensions, stone recordings, planetary measurements or acoustic properties.

Before editing, locate the complete Godot project, its engine version, main scene, assets, export presets and existing tests. A source overlay is not a complete project; an exported Web folder is not the editable source. Reconcile the installed build with the candidate and preserve the last working installation.

## Implementation rules

- Map geometry and mathematical structures to explicit data, algorithms and testable invariants, rather than decorative terminology.
- Preserve the requested formulas and precision domains. Keep rendering conversions outside designated exact kernels and document their interfaces.
- Do not remove collision, articulation, sound propagation, material variation or destruction behaviour to make a performance number look better. Preserve every required equation, term, coupling, mode, domain, initial condition and boundary condition without approximation, reduction, truncation, transformed formulations or replacement formulas.
- Treat authored movement tuning as tuning, not measured biomechanics. Treat procedural sound as synthesis, not a recording of a real stone.
- Keep movement, visual animation, collision events and audio event ownership coherent. Do not let separate agents independently alter shared timing or coordinate conventions.
- Deliver code only, without code comments, commentary docstrings, explanations or separate explanatory artifacts. Keep necessary verification records internal; disclose material blockers and failed or incomplete work briefly when required.

## Required acceptance evidence

Choose measurable tolerances before implementing the change. Record the test environment, timestep, units, inputs and source revision. Preserve the existing suite; add regressions that fail on the original defect.

Movement and animation checks must include start/stop, turning, slopes, stairs, jump/landing, carrying and multiple physics rates. For planted feet, measure toe position and orientation in world coordinates, not only an ankle anchor. Check transitions and camera behaviour with ordinary user input.

Audio checks must cover collision onset, material identity, spatial position, variation, clipping, overlapping events, settled rubble and object teardown. Verify that resting debris does not repeatedly retrigger impact sound. Listen to the actual rendered output; event counters cannot establish natural sound.

Destruction checks must cover fragment transforms, mass/inertia assumptions, collision stability, sound placement and settled-state behaviour. State which quantities are physically modelled and which are authored.

Run import, affected tests, the existing aggregate suite and Web export on the final combined revision. Then test the extracted deliverable through its documented launch route: initial focus, ordinary input, pointer capture/release, pause/resume, reload, missing resources and console errors. Inspect visuals and audio, not just a successful process exit.
