# Evidence-led monetisation instructions

Status: operating framework for research and experiments, not a current valuation, price recommendation, revenue forecast or authorization to contact customers.

## Define what can be sold

Separate four assets: demonstrated software capability, mathematical results with verifiable proofs/certificates, validated datasets or workflows, and research hypotheses. Ancient mathematical or geometric inspiration belongs in the provenance and design account; it is not proof of novelty, ownership, efficacy or commercial advantage.

For each proposed offering, name one buyer role, one costly problem, the exact delivered outcome, required inputs, operating constraints and evidence of value. State which components exist and which still require development. Do not sell a research roadmap as a functioning product.

For GRVQ/MSTVQ/TGCR and related solvers, specify the mathematical task before discussing architecture or value. Compare against a named classical method on the same objective, constraints, instance set, accuracy requirement and resource accounting. Return exact certificates where the problem and implementation support them, together with verification cost, objective-level results, runtime, memory and hardware. Workflow routing alone is not a distinctive mathematical advantage.

## Claims and benchmark gate

- Keep claims about exact arithmetic, quantum execution, hybrid execution, speed, accuracy and physical validity separate. State whether quantum hardware, a simulator or only a classical component actually ran.
- Do not claim quantum advantage, universal superiority, commercial readiness or a protective moat without the corresponding evidence.
- Use reproducible datasets, versioned code, declared baselines, fair tuning budgets and recorded failures. Include preparation, compilation, communication, sampling and verification costs when they affect the buyer's task.
- Separate training/tuning data from held-out evaluation. Report the tested distribution and limits; do not generalise a small favourable case to all tasks.
- Preserve full mathematical and functional scope. Do not relax the baseline's target or weaken the proposed method's accuracy to create a marketing win.
- Record rights to code, third-party libraries, recordings, models, datasets and branding. Historical provenance does not establish intellectual-property clearance. Obtain specialist review where legal certainty is needed.

## Evidence-gated experiments

1. Capability inventory: produce a versioned matrix of working features, proofs, tests, limits and missing dependencies. Gate: another person can reproduce the claimed outcome from the delivered artifact.
2. Problem discovery: draft questions for a specific buyer category about current workflow, cost, failure modes and acceptance criteria. Gate: observed demand evidence, not enthusiasm inferred from a demonstration. Outreach requires authorization.
3. Demonstration: select a narrow, representative task with a named baseline and an intelligible result. Gate: the demo uses the real implementation and clearly labels any illustrative material.
4. Pilot specification: define inputs, deliverables, success thresholds, runtime/support limits, responsibilities and data handling before a pilot. Gate: a testable scope and authorized counterpart; no unapproved service or payment commitments.
5. Pricing experiment: compare clearly defined packaging hypotheses, such as a reproducible analysis deliverable, a licensed application or a supported integration. Record willingness-to-pay evidence, support cost and delivery cost. Do not invent a price from lines of code, sunk effort or a broad market-size estimate.
6. Repeatability: verify that a second representative case meets the same acceptance criteria without hidden bespoke repair. Gate: sustainable delivery evidence before recurring promises or scaling claims.

## Project packaging

Cymatica offers must identify the exact simulation model, validated parameter range, hardware requirements, export capabilities and limitations. ETERNAL offers must distinguish a playable demonstration from a finished game and identify rights to all assets. A research licence must distinguish access to code from a warranty of physical or commercial results.

Track evidence in a claim ledger: claim, artifact/revision, test or source, reviewer, date, limitations and allowed wording. Recheck current product/pricing/legal facts from authoritative sources when an actual decision is requested. Keep speculative valuation and scenarios explicitly separate from demonstrated earnings or an independent valuation.

Finish with a bounded next experiment and its pass/fail decision, not an unsupported revenue prediction. Marketing, publication, sales outreach, contracts and payments remain subject to the user's authorization and applicable approval requirements.
