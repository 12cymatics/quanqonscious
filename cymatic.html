What this is. An exact solution of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations coupled to a finite-strain elastic vessel and a pinned liquid–air surface, on the interval 5/4 ≤ s ≤ 3/2. Every field is closed-form and every local residual below is exactly zero over the rationals — not small, zero.

What it is not. OpenAI's Navier–Stokes result is a finite-time breakdown construction: for each viscosity it produces a smooth compactly supported force, chosen from the constructed flow's own momentum residual, under which a resting whole-space flow has bounded energy and unbounded velocity as t → 1. That force is not this apparatus's actuator, the theorem supplies no vessel or free-surface solution, and it does not say this cup blows up. Nothing here reproduces it. This benchmark's prescribed body forces and flat free surface are also not the centre-driven Faraday problem the renderer above shows, which is why the two are kept apart.

Layers are kept separate. Fold labels are the paper's printed classifications. Contrast / centre-bias / ring / texture figures are image descriptors measured from the rendered PDF panels — renderer hints, never the authors' measurements. Bessel modes and water properties are theory, used for diagnostics and outside the atlas.
Amplitude stays in apparatus mV: the paper gives no mV→acceleration transfer function, so none is invented. Surface tension is IAPWS R1-76; viscosity is the Vogel–Andrade fit, not IAPWS.
