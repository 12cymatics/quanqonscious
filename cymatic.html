The grains are simulated, not shaded

A grain on a vibrating plate gets thrown into the air wherever the surface is moving, lands somewhere slightly different, and repeats — a random walk whose step size is set by the local amplitude. Where the plate is still, it stops. The net effect is a drift down the gradient of the squared displacement, and that is what each of the forty-five thousand grains here integrates:

v ← (v − D·u∇u + ξ·|u|)·λ     x ← x + v

Nodal lines are where the grains end up, not where the renderer was told to draw a bright line. That is why the pattern takes a moment to form, why grains keep trembling on the antinodes, and why turning the frequency scatters them before they resettle.

The drift needs ∇u, and it is available in closed form rather than by differencing the grid: ∂u/∂r = k·J′m(kr)·cos(mθ) and (1/r)·∂u/∂θ = −m·k·[Jm(kr)/kr]·sin(mθ), with J′m = (Jm−1 − Jm+1)/2. The bracket Jm(x)/x is what would divide by zero at the centre; its limit is exactly 1/2 for m = 1 and 0 for m ≥ 2, so it is evaluated, not fudged. Checked against central differences: agreement to 1.8e−11, which is the differencing error, not the formula's.

Where the frequencies come from

Separating ∇²u + k²u = 0 on a disc gives u = Jm(kr)·cos(mθ). Clamping the rim at radius a forces Jm(ka) = 0, so the admissible wavenumbers are k = jm,n/a — the n-th zero of Jm. Those zeros set the spectrum, and their ratios are not whole numbers, which is why a drum has no harmonic series.

Jm comes from its integral form (1/2π)∫−ππ cos(mτ − x sin τ)dτ by the trapezoid rule on a full period — an analytic periodic integrand, so the rule converges spectrally and there is no truncation order to tune. The zeros follow by Newton from McMahon's estimate.

The point count was measured, not guessed: 64 points looked converged on small arguments and was still wrong by 8.8e−7 at x = 40. This uses 128.

The bug this replaces

The engine this came from, vedic_cymatic_pde_engine.py, built its radius like this:

r = safe_inverse_distance(r_sq)  →  1 / (r² + 1e-12)
r = 1 / r  →  r² + 1e-12

The variable named r held r squared, and an epsilon was added on every evaluation regardless of where the point was. That value was then passed to Jn and used as the denominator of cos θ. At the lattice point (3,4) the mode value should be 0.3977; the old code returned −43.70.

There was never a singularity to suppress — it was manufactured by dividing by r to get cos θ. Nothing here divides: at the centre Jm(0) = 0 for every m ≥ 1, which annihilates the undefined angle by itself.
